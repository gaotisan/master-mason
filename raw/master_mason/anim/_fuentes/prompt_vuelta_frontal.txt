PROMPT PARA EL VIDEO DE LA VUELTA (de frente a de espaldas, con el baston)
=========================================================================
(Google Flow / Veo, "Frames to Video", 2026-10-07)

Para enlazar andar_frente y andar_espalda (scenes/dev/prueba_frontal.tscn):
el peregrino viene hacia la camara, se da la vuelta en el sitio y se va.

  - fotograma inicial: referencia_vuelta_frente.png  (andar_frente c_018, con la
    garra de cuernos ya puesta en el baston, sin bola)
  - fotograma final:   referencia_vuelta_espalda.png (andar_espalda c_005)
  Los dos a escala y sitio de las demas referencias (capucha-pies 628, pies en
  la fila 879, centrado en x 959, verde #24D732). Copiados en Descargas.
  - 16:9, 8 s, el modelo de mas calidad. 2-4 tomas.

Que mirar en cada toma, por orden:
  1. el baston SIEMPRE en su mano derecha (de frente a la izquierda de la
     pantalla, de espaldas a la derecha) y con la garra de cuernos: que no se
     la cambie de mano ni la garra se convierta en punta
  2. que la vuelta dure poco (1-1,5 s) con dos pasos claros, a la velocidad del
     juego: acelerar despues se ve a saltitos
  3. camara quieta, mismo tamano, sin desplazarse por la pantalla
  4. la tunica: por delante es de parches y por detras lleva el chevron gris;
     que el paso de una a otra no se vea raro a mitad
  5. sin sombra en el suelo (cuesta mucho limpiarla)
  6. la capucha: en la primera version salia como un gorro de mago con punta;
     el prompt ya no dice "pointed" y la describe como capucha de tela caida
Si sale bien pero va hacia el otro lado, sirve igual (se puede espejar si el
baston queda en la mano buena).

---------

Locked-off static camera, no camera movement at all: no pan, no zoom, no dolly,
no tilt, no cuts. Flat pure chroma-key green background (#24D732) filling the
whole frame, no shadows, no cast shadow on the ground, no floor line, no props,
no text, no logos, no on-screen interface or player controls.

The old pilgrim from the first frame faces the camera, holding his tall
wooden staff with the open antler-shaped claw at the top in his right hand. He
stands still for two seconds. Then, in one single quick and natural movement,
he turns around in place to face away from the camera, the way an old man turns
back to walk the way he came: the whole turn takes only about one second, a
pivot with just two short, clear steps of his bare feet, without stopping
halfway and without extra small steps. The staff stays in his right hand the
whole time, upright, moving with his body; the antler claw keeps its shape.
His position on screen and his size do not change, his head stays at the same
height. He ends seen exactly from behind, identical to the last frame, and holds
completely still until the end of the video.

His head is covered by the soft grey cloth hood of his cloak: low and
rounded, draped loosely over his head and falling onto his shoulders, exactly
the shape of the first frame. It is part of the cloak, not a hat: no tall
point, no cone, no wizard hat, the hood never grows or stands up.

Same character throughout: the soft grey cloth hood, white beard (seen from the
front), brown faceted patchwork cloak, the grey chevron patch on the back of
the cloak, thin dark legs and bare feet. Same low-poly flat-shaded
hand-painted style, same colours and lighting as the first frame, crisp frames
with no motion blur, soft matte texture, no flicker. Sound: only the rustle of
the cloak and two light footsteps, no music, no voice.
